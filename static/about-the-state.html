Geography

The single town of Edinburgh lies on Tristan da Cunha - a remote island forming part of Britain's overseas territory alongside Saint Helena plus Ascension Island.

Tristan da Cunha, an inhabited island about 11 kilometers across and covers 98 square kilometers.

The primary isle sheltered 250 people by fall 2018 - each a citizen of the British territories.

Political system

On Tristan, folks don't organize into either political groups or labor organizations. Instead, power rests with the King - who sends a governor to run things there.

Until 2009, Tristan da Cunha functioned under Saint Helena reign - the governor there spoke for them too.

Eight people win seats on the Island Council, while three others are chosen to join them. Everyone serves for three years, starting either in February or March.

Flora and Fauna

Plants and animals throughout these islands also appear across a wide area encompassing the Southern Atlantic along with the Southern Pacific.

Folks come to Tristan mostly to see the animals. BirdLife International designated it a key spot for birds - it's home to thirteen kinds that nest along the coast, alongside a couple living at the hinterland all year round.

Many kinds of whales and dolphins - show up near Tristan from time to time, encounters becoming more frequent.

Historical places

Far placed from others, St. Joseph Church stands as a Roman Catholic parish - perhaps the most distant one globally. A chapel once occupied this location, yet gave way to the current church building during the 90s.

St. Mary's Church: built in 1923 and serves as a place of worship withing the Church of England. Was later renovated and extended in 1990 to attune the community. The church was officially opened in July of 2025.
